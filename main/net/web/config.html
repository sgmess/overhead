Radar

Swipe up on the scope to see further, down to see closer.

Altitude is in hundreds of feet with a climb or descent arrow; speed is ground speed in knots.

Where each of the nearest 50 aircraft has been, fading with age.

Military aircraft get a pink diamond. Routes come from adsb.im, which is sent the callsign of the aircraft you tap; a question mark means the route doesn't fit where it is. The flight number, airline and flag of the country of registration need no lookup. Helicopters are always drawn with a rotor.

Filter

The kind of aircraft is what its transponder says it is; many military aircraft, and some others, don't say. The altitude limits leave aircraft on the ground, and any without an altitude, alone. An aircraft in an emergency always shows. While anything is hidden the count reads "12 of 30 aircraft".

Alerts

The aircraft blinks amber and a banner names it; tap the banner for its card. Altitude is pressure altitude, so near an airport set the ceiling with that in mind.

Squawk 7700 (emergency), 7600 (radio failure) or 7500 (hijack), or an emergency the aircraft reports over ADS-B (medical, low fuel). A red banner gives the code and the aircraft, ahead of any overhead alert.

Each aircraft chimes once. The screen comes up to full brightness at night while an alert lasts.

Map layers

Airspace and airfields from OpenAIP, for 107 NM around the centre, refreshed daily. They need a free OpenAIP account and API key, created on openaip.net under your profile's API clients.

OpenAIP data is fetched a country at a time. Left empty, it's the country of the airfield nearest the centre; near a border, list the neighbours as well, e.g. FR,CH.

Flight details

With an AeroDataBox key (direct API access, which can run on credits earned by feeding ADS-B), the aircraft you pick on the screen shows when its flight left and gets in and how late it is, the flight number it's sold under, today's route, and the aircraft's full type, age and seats. Only the aircraft you tap is looked up: 2 credits for its flight, sometimes 4 (its times again after 15 minutes if you are still looking), and 1 for the aircraft, each kept until a restart.
